
## 0.Project Initial Brefing and Content 
Benchmarking the Intelligent Virtual Agent SDK as a Research Platform for Doppelgängers


This project evaluates the Intelligent Virtual Agent SDK as a platform for building controlled virtual human experiments. The student will implement several minimal scripted agent behaviours and compare them in terms of setup effort, runtime stability, behaviour latency, and perceived quality. The outcome should be a practical assessment of whether the SDK is suitable for future XR and virtual human studies. [uhhhci/intelligent-virtual-agent-sdk](https://github.com/uhhhci/intelligent-virtual-agent-sdk)
- Objective: Evaluate the capabilities of IVH SDK across scenarios. What are usage boundaries? What can it do? Developer-focused evaluation.
- Method: Criteria selection; implementation of reference scenarios and demonstrators; evaluation with developers (ease of use,...); performance evaluation
- Outcomes: A critical discussion of the toolkit around usage scenarios for doppelgängers and recommendation of for future use case

## 1. Project Introduction

**Title: Evaluating the Intelligent Virtual Agent SDK as a Research Platform for Controlled Doppelgänger Experiments in XR**


## 2. Research Question

**Main RQ** To what extent is the IVA SDK suitable as a research platform for controlled doppelgänger experiments in XR?
- **RQ1** What doppelgänger-relevant capabilities does the SDK support, and where are its practical boundaries?
- **RQ2** What are the trade-offs and constraint-enforcement limitations of the SDK across the human–AI autonomy spectrum for doppelgänger behaviour?
- **RQ3** What is the developer experience of using the SDK for this doppelgänger research use case?


## 3. Literature Review — Brief Summary

The literature review is organised around three pillars that together motivate this
dissertation's three research sub-questions.

**Pillar 1 (Doppelgängers & identity in XR)** shows that identity substitution in
shared VR is both perceptually unstable and increasingly used as a deliberate design
feature (e.g. Oliva et al.'s 2026 avatar-swap study; Senel et al.'s change-blindness
findings; Bai et al.'s SEAM stand-in system). This establishes *why* doppelgänger-style
agents are a legitimate and timely research object — motivating RQ1 and RQ2.

**Pillar 2 (Control paradigms for virtual agents)** reviews the spectrum from
Wizard-of-Oz (fully human-controlled) through constrained-AI to fully autonomous
LLM-driven agents, drawing on WOz tooling literature (Bellucci et al.) and recent
embodied-agent WOz studies, alongside the SDK's own LLM-driven architecture. This
supplies the rationale for RQ2's three demonstration conditions across the autonomy
spectrum.

**Pillar 3 (HCI toolkit evaluation)** establishes the methodological basis for the
whole dissertation: Ledo et al.'s (2018) four evaluation strategies, which the SDK's
own authors (Li et al., 2026) already used to evaluate themselves — giving this
project both a validated framework (RQ1, RQ3) and a prior baseline to compare against.

**The gap this dissertation addresses**: doppelgänger-style AI agents are increasingly
studied as a phenomenon, and toolkit evaluation methodology exists, but no independent
study has yet applied a structured, Ledo-style evaluation specifically to assess
whether a current-generation LLM-driven IVA SDK is fit for controlled doppelgänger
research across the human-control-to-autonomous spectrum. This is the gap RQ0 (and its sub-questions RQ1–RQ3) fills.
